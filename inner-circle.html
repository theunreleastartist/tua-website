What this actually is
A gym doesn't teach you how to lift. It gives you a reason to show up, and other people who notice when you don't.

You already know most of what you need to know. You have watched the videos. You have read the threads. The problem was never information. The problem is that finishing something is lonely, and nobody is waiting for it, so it stays at ninety percent forever.

This is the other thing. There is no curriculum to fall behind on, no modules to complete, no homework. There is a room, a rhythm, and a group of people who all know what you said you were going to finish.

You bring the work. The room brings the reason.

The ninety-day cycle

Ninety days. One thing finished.

Every cycle has the same three movements. You name the thing, you get unstuck, you put it out.

01
Commit
Month one. You name one real thing — the EP, the mix, the first ten clients, the record you have been circling for two years. You say it out loud in the room and it stops being a private idea.
Live call · one hour
02
Unstick
Month two. This is where it always goes wrong, and this is the month most people quit alone. You bring the actual blocker — the doubt, the scope, the thing you keep avoiding — and we work it in front of everyone.
Live call · one hour
03
Ship
Month three. It goes out. Released, delivered, sent, published — done and out of your hands. Not perfect. Out. Then we look at what it cost you and what the next ninety days should hold.
Live call · one hour

Every call is recorded. Miss one and you are not behind — there is nothing to be behind on.

Honest about what this isn't
What the Inner Circle doesn't include.
Some things belong in a deeper container. These are them.
1:1 sessions

The sessions, the accountability one-to-one, me embedded in your specific project — that is the Thinking Partnership. The Inner Circle is a room, not a private line.

A curriculum

There are no modules, no lessons, no course to complete. That is deliberate. If there were a syllabus you could fall behind on it, and falling behind is the thing that makes people quit.

A guarantee that you'll finish

Nobody can promise that. What the room can do is make it much harder to quietly disappear — and that turns out to be most of the difference.

Direct personal access to me

I am in the room, I read every check-in, and I am on every call. What I can't be is on the other end of a message at eleven at night. That is what 1:1 is for.

Questions

The things people ask.

What if I cannot start in January?
Then you take the next cycle. They run back to back all year, and the waitlist gets first refusal on every one — so put your name down now and pick the cycle that actually fits your year. Nobody joins halfway through: the whole point is that the room moves together.
So this is a program, not a subscription?
It's both, and the gym is the honest comparison. You pay monthly and you can leave whenever you want. What you get for the money isn't content — it's a room, a rhythm, and people who notice. The ninety-day cycle is the shape of the training, not a course with an end date.
How long do the cohorts run?
Ninety days each, back to back, all year. Every cohort is capped and starts together — twelve places. The founding twelve open in January, and the waitlist gets first refusal on them.
Is this the same as working with you 1:1?
No. The Inner Circle is a room. The Thinking Partnership is a chair opposite me. In the Inner Circle I am present, I read everything, and I am on the calls — but the attention is shared. If you want the work done on you rather than alongside you, book a discovery call.
I'm an engineer, not an artist. Is this for me?
Yes. Mix engineers, mastering engineers, producers, songwriters, people building a studio or a service business around music — the thing that stalls is the same thing regardless of the job title. Half the value in the room comes from being around people whose work isn't identical to yours.
